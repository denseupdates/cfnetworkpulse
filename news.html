How Many Athletes Qualify for the CrossFit Games Out of the Torian Pro?

The Torian Pro will send a sizable group of athletes to the CrossFit Games this season, with qualification spots spread across elite, team, and age-group divisions.[file:21]

On the elite side, the top 3 men, top 3 women, and top 3 teams on the final Torian Pro leaderboard will each earn a place at the Games.

In the age-group field, the 35-39 and 40-44 divisions will each qualify 3 athletes, while the 45-49 and 50-54 divisions will each send 2.

The 55-59, 60-64, 65-69, and 70-plus divisions will each award 1 qualifying spot, making every place around the cut line important as the weekend unfolds.

To make that easier to follow, the leaderboard includes a clearly marked “CF Games Qualifier” cut line that shows exactly who is currently in a qualifying position and who is just outside the bubble.

As scores update throughout the weekend, that cut line will be one of the easiest ways to track the race for Games spots in every division.

For all the direct links to Torian Pro leaderboards and live streams, see this article: How To Watch This Weekend’s Torian Pro .

Top Story May 17, 2026

French Throwdown To The 2026 CrossFit Games

French Throwdown handed out nine more tickets to the 2026 CrossFit Games this weekend in Paris, with Jayson Hopper, Moritz Fiebig, Nika Maisuradze, Mirjam von Rohr, Aimee Cringle, Elisa Fuliano, CrossFit Oslo Kriger, CrossFit Sodra Vattern, and Team Butchers Lab Pursuit all qualifying for San Jose.

There were six scored events across the weekend, with Event 5 split into two parts, Part A and Part B. Both parts of Event 5 were worth fifty points each to the winners, instead of the usual one hundred points for a single event, which made every placing even more important in the final standings.

On the mens side Hopper won the field with 554 points after six scored events, including two event wins and four top two finishes, even with a ninth place on Part B of the fifth event. The split format in Event 5 meant one bad finish did not completely erase a strong finish, and Hopper had already built enough momentum to stay in control of the leaderboard through the close.

Fiebig locked up second with 467 points, while Maisuradze grabbed the final qualifying spot with 466, just one point behind Fiebig and twenty nine points clear of fourth place Luis Cuellar. That podium finish gave Maisuradze his first Rookie CrossFit Games ticket and capped one of the tighter mens races of the weekend.

The womens leaderboard stayed tight all weekend. Mirjam von Rohr took the overall win with 526 points, Aimee Cringle finished second with 513, and Elisa Fuliano claimed the third and final Games spot with 470.

With the same six event format and the split fifth event, Fulianos consistency across the weekend mattered, and she ultimately edged Leah Storen by only four points for the last qualifying position by getting enough people between her and Leah in that final event.

In the team division CrossFit Oslo Kriger controlled the competition from the opening event and backed it up with four wins out of the five scored tests. Oslo finished on 464 points, sixty points ahead of CrossFit Sodra Vattern in second, while Team Butchers Lab Pursuit secured the third and final qualifying position with 392 points.

For all nine qualifiers the French Throwdown weekend was more than a big payday. With six events across three days and Games spots on the line, every point mattered, and the athletes and teams that survived Paris now turn their focus to the 2026 CrossFit Games in San Jose.

Top Story May 13, 2026

Syndicate Crown Workouts Released

Syndicate Crown has dropped the full slate of individual and team workouts, and Taylor Self and Sevan Matossian were the first to go live breaking them down for fans, walking through stimulus, and how top athletes may strategize the weekend. The full test for individuals, Ball Till You Fall, Combo Climb, Bicarb, Semifinal Nate, Trip Advisory, and Perfect Square, can be seen in the released workout graphic above, giving athletes and coaches their first complete look at what they will face on the floor.

On the programming side, Syndicate once again taps JR Howell of CrossFit Crash as mastermind, marking his second straight year designing the tests for this semifinal. Howell has earned a growing reputation for creating balanced, brutal but fair weekends that reward well rounded fitness over gimmicks. The combination of JR Howell and Syndicate organizer Wilson Pak again has expectations high for another tightly run, highly competitive weekend.

On the men’s side, there are three coveted Games spots on the line and a loaded field of contenders. Colten Mertens, Saxon Panchik, Nate Ackerman, Chris Ibarra, and Austin Hatfield headline the list of big name athletes expected to be in the mix for the podium, each bringing slightly different strengths into a very complete test.

The women’s division is just as stacked, with three Games spots available and several established names circling Syndicate Crown as their gateway back to the big show. Kyra Milligan, Danielle Brandon, Alexis Raptis, Lydia Fish, and Anikha Greer all look poised to make serious runs at the top of the leaderboard.

Teams also have three Games tickets up for grabs, and the race there is wide open. Squads like CrossFit Whip, CrossFit Mayhem, TTT CrossFit, and CrossFit 1124 are all firmly in the conversation as potential qualifiers, each bringing a mix of Games experience and deep rosters into the weekend.
