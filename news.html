Jayson Hopper Withdraws From Mayhem Classic Due to Groin Injury

Jayson Hopper has withdrawn from the Mayhem Classic due to a groin injury. The Mayhem Classic posted to their story his name card and custom recovery mat for the event suggesting he is no longer competing this weekend. CF Network News has confirmed that he is still not healed from the groin injury that Hopper shared on his Instagram story, and at this time it is unclear whether he will be healthy in time to compete at any in person semifinals this season.

Given the nature of the injury and the current timeline, the safest option for Hopper is to prioritize recovery and target the online semifinals beginning June 11th. Focusing on healing rather than rushing back to the competition floor should give him the best chance at qualifying for the CrossFit Games to defend his title.

We will keep you updated as more information about his recovery and competition plans becomes available.

Source: Jayson Hopper Instagram story, CF Network News

Competition April 15, 2026

Mayhem Classic Confirms Event 2 "Gwen"

The Mayhem Classic has announced Event 2 and, as some predicted, it is the classic CrossFit benchmark Gwen. The event will feature a 15-12-9 rep scheme of clean and jerks at 185 pounds for the men and 125 pounds for the women, with all sets required to be unbroken, matching the original benchmark intent.

This reveal confirms the earlier guess from CF Network News as well as from Taylor Self, owner and head programmer of Sentinel Training, who shared a screenshot of a conversation where he had also predicted Gwen would appear on the Mayhem Classic floor.

Even though Gwen will not close out the weekend as the final event, it is shaping up to be one of the most exciting tests on the schedule. Heavy unbroken barbell cycling at these loads will put a premium on barbell proficiency, composure under fatigue and intelligent rest between sets, and a single failed rep can derail an athlete’s entire score on this workout.

From a broader CrossFit perspective, Gwen is also a nod to the journal era idea that a heavy, high rep clean and jerk is one of the most complete single movement tests in the sport. A touch and go clean and jerk at challenging loading demands and develops all 10 general physical skills in the CrossFit methodology; cardiorespiratory endurance, stamina, strength, flexibility, power, speed, coordination, agility, balance and accuracy, making this event a powerful standalone assessment of overall fitness rather than just a weightlifting showcase.

Source: Mayhem Classic announcement, Taylor Self Instagram story

Competition April 14, 2026

Mayhem Classic Reveals Event 5 "Froning's Benchmark"

The Mayhem Classic has officially revealed Event 5, aptly named Froning’s Benchmark, and it is exactly what you would expect from a Rich Froning inspired test. The workout includes upper body pulling, moderately heavy front squats and high skill handstand walking with obstacles.

The rope climb standard is already drawing criticism because the men will perform two legless rope climbs each round, while the women will do one legless and one regular rope climb each round, reducing their total legless volume. In the comments of a Barbell Spin post, HWPO coach Justin Cotler argued that it is 2026 and that the sport should stop scaling women’s high skill movements at the Semifinal and Games level, and for obvious reasons because his athlete Alex Gazan is very strong in upper body pulling and would likely benefit from the full legless volume.

There is still at least one more event to be announced and it does not appear that Event 5 will serve as the final, so it is possible there will be an Event 6. There is also speculation that one workout will be programmed at every in person semifinal, and there are rumors that it could be CrossFit benchmark workout "Gwen", which would make for a very exciting closing test because the 15-12-9 rep scheme of clean and jerks at 185 for the men and 125 for the ladies must be completed in unbroken sets.

Source: Mayhem Classic announcement, Barbell Spin comments and community speculation

Competition April 13, 2026

Dave Castro Comments on Jay Crouch Penalty as PRVN Pushes Back

This afternoon on Dave Castro’s most recent Week in Review on YouTube, he addressed the penalty given to Jay Crouch for his Workout 1 score. Castro said Crouch did not complete one overhead squat rep, though he did not specify whether the missed rep came in the first or second set of overhead squats. Castro also said there was a second issue being that the video submitted to the CrossFit review team included a clock overlaid on the footage, meaning it was an edited video. Castro said the one minute penalty was appropriate.

A clipped version of Castro’s comments was later posted to the Dense Updates Instagram page, where PRVN CEO Nic Johnston commented, “This isn’t true,” publicly disputing at least part of the account now circulating around the penalty and video submission.

Source: Dave Castro YouTube Week in Review, Dense Updates Instagram comments
